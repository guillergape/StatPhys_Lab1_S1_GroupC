\begin{abstract}
We study a system of $N$ fair, independent coins, the simplest two-state model of statistical mechanics (isomorphic to a spin-$1/2$ paramagnet or a binary lattice gas). Two computational routes are used: (i) full bitwise enumeration of the $2^N$ microstates with constant memory, which reaches $N_{\max}=40$ within a 5-minute wall-clock limit; and (ii) log-space combinatorics with the log-Gamma function, which avoids the float64 overflow of $\Omega$ beyond $N=1029$ and is complemented by Stirling/Gaussian asymptotics for $N\sim10^{23}$. For our team size $N=100+M=121$ we compute macrostate probabilities, interval probabilities and Boltzmann entropy differences $\Delta S=k_B\ln[\Omega(k_1)/\Omega(k_2)]$. Relative fluctuations decay as $\sigma/N=1/(2\sqrt N)$, and the waiting times of ordered states show that entropy decreases are allowed but, for macroscopic $N$, never observed: the statistical Second Law becomes deterministic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction \& Objectives}
\label{sec:intro}

Consider $N$ distinguishable, non-interacting coins, each heads ($\sigma_i=1$) or tails ($\sigma_i=0$). A \textbf{microstate} $\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_N)\in\{0,1\}^N$ fixes every coin; there are $2^N$ of them and, by the fundamental postulate, each has probability $2^{-N}$. A \textbf{macrostate} is fixed only by the number of heads $k=\sum_i\sigma_i\in\{0,\dots,N\}$; its multiplicity and probability are
\begin{equation}
    \Omega(N,k)=\binom{N}{k}=\frac{N!}{k!\,(N-k)!},\qquad P(N,k)=\frac{\Omega(N,k)}{2^N},
    \label{eq:binomial_multiplicity}
\end{equation}
and its Boltzmann entropy is $S(N,k)=k_B\ln\Omega(N,k)$, with $k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}$\,J/K. For $N\to\infty$ the de~Moivre--Laplace theorem turns the binomial into a Gaussian,
\begin{equation}
    \Omega(N,k)\simeq2^N\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi N}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{2\,(k-N/2)^2}{N}\right],
    \label{eq:gaussian_limit}
\end{equation}
of mean $N/2$ and width $\sigma=\sqrt N/2$: the absolute spread grows, but the relative one, $\sigma/N=1/(2\sqrt N)$, vanishes ($\sim10^{-12}$ for $N\sim10^{23}$).

Following the lab manual \cite{tribaldos}, we enumerate the microstates (Part~1a,~b, Section~\ref{sec:microstates}), compute $\Omega(N,k)$ in log space up to $N\sim10^{23}$ (Part~1c--e), and, for $N=100+M$, compute probabilities, entropy differences and waiting times and discuss the Second Law and the limit $N\to\infty$ (Part~2, Section~\ref{sec:thermodynamics}).

\section{Methodology \& Collaborative Workflow}
\label{sec:methodology}

\textbf{Environment.} The Conda environment \texttt{statphys\_lab1} (\texttt{environment.yml}) pins Python 3.11 (for \texttt{int.bit\_count}), NumPy $\ge2.0$ (for \texttt{bitwise\_count}), SciPy $\ge1.10$ (for \texttt{gammaln}), Matplotlib $\ge3.7$ (vector PDF figures) and pytest $\ge7.0$.

\textbf{Code.} The package \texttt{src/} has one module per topic (\texttt{microstates.py}: enumeration; \texttt{combinatorics.py}: $\ln\Omega$; \texttt{thermodynamics.py}: probabilities, entropy, large $N$), coded against function signatures agreed in \texttt{PLAN.md} before any branch started. \texttt{main.py} runs every question in order, prints the results quoted in this report and regenerates both figures.

\textbf{Git workflow.} The lab was split into eleven phases with an owner each. Every phase was developed on its own feature branch (e.g.\ \texttt{feature/log-combinatorics}) and merged into \texttt{main} only through a pull request approved by a designated teammate; nobody commits to \texttt{main} directly.

\textbf{Testing.} The \texttt{tests/} suite checks the invariants $\sum_k\Omega(N,k)=2^N$, $\Omega(N,k)=\Omega(N,N-k)$, $\Omega(N,0)=\Omega(N,N)=1$, $\Omega(N,1)=N$, and $e^{\ln\Omega}$ against the exact \texttt{math.comb(N, k)} for all $k$ and $N\le100$ (relative error $<10^{-11}$); every new function was added with its tests.

\textbf{AI assistance.} Claude Code, GitHub Copilot and Google Antigravity were used as assistants. Every query is logged in \texttt{AI\_STATEMENT.md} (prompt, what was generated, what the team changed and how it was verified); Appendix~\ref{sec:ai_statement} summarises it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Full enumeration is limited by time, not memory: generating states on the fly keeps memory constant, but the work doubles with each coin, so 5 minutes reach only $N_{\max}=36$ (i7) to $40$ (M4 Max), and a $180\times$ speed-up buys $7.5$ coins. Computing $\ln\Omega$ with log-Gamma avoids the float64 overflow of $\Omega$ at $N=1030$ and stays accurate up to $N\sim10^{13}$; Stirling's formula covers $N\sim10^{23}$. For our $N=121$ the probability concentrates around $N/2$ ($93.14\,\%$ within $\pm10$ heads, $99.98\,\%$ within $\pm20$ tails), entropy decreases of $1.5$--$10\,k_B$ are ordinary fluctuations, and a state with 8 heads takes $\approx6.8\times10^6$ ages of the Universe. No result contradicts the Second Law: entropy decreases have relative probability $e^{\Delta S/k_B}$, frequent for small $N$ but suppressed as $e^{-\mathcal O(N)}$ for $N\sim10^{23}$, where the relative width $\sigma/N\sim10^{-12}$ vanishes and the statistical law becomes deterministic.
